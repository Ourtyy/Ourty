"Coffee lowers dementia risk!" If that headline has popped up in your feed this month, you're not alone.

It comes from a huge Harvard study published in JAMA that followed more than 130,000 people for up to 43 years. People who drank two to three cups of caffeinated coffee a day had about an 18% lower risk of dementia. Tea drinkers saw a smaller benefit, and decaf showed no link at all. That's encouraging news for coffee lovers. But it's not quite the same as "coffee prevents dementia."

**Three ways to enjoy your coffee wisely:**

- **Stick to a sensible amount.** The benefit peaked at 2–3 cups a day. More didn't add anything.
- **Protect your sleep.** Have your last cup earlier in the day.
- **Go easy on the extras.** The study looked at coffee and tea, not sugary coffee-shop drinks.

The full article explains what the study can and can't prove, who should be careful with caffeine, and the everyday habits that do the most for your brain.
