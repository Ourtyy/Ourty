Every night, thousands of people stick a strip of tape over their lips before bed. Social media promises deeper sleep, less snoring, fresher breath and even a sharper jawline. It sounds cheap and harmless, but is it?

The research is small and mostly unconvincing. Reviews of the available studies found little support for mouth taping as a sleep treatment, with only modest effects on snoring for some people. And doctors warn that the people most drawn to it, heavy snorers and mouth breathers, may have a blocked nose or sleep apnea that tape could hide or even make risky.

**Three gentler ways to breathe and sleep easier:**

- **Sleep on your side.** Lying on your back makes snoring more likely.
- **Clear your nose.** Nasal strips or treating allergies and congestion can help air flow.
- **Get checked.** Loud snoring, gasping or waking up exhausted are worth mentioning to your doctor.

The full article covers what the studies found, who should never try mouth taping, the risks influencers don't mention and when to see a doctor.
