"Exercise more" is classic advice for bad nights. But which kind of exercise actually helps most?

A new review pooled 30 trials with more than 2,500 adults who sleep poorly and compared walking, aerobic workouts, strength training, tai chi and yoga. The winner surprised a lot of people: short, energetic yoga sessions twice a week came out on top, with walking and strength training close behind.

The evidence is still limited, so yoga isn't a magic cure. But it's a good reminder that regular movement and better sleep go hand in hand.

**Three easy ways to move for better sleep:**

- **Try two short, active yoga sessions a week.** A 20–30 minute flow that warms you up is enough.
- **Walk on most other days.** Even 10–15 brisk minutes counts, especially in morning daylight.
- **Keep evenings gentle.** Finish harder workouts well before bedtime and save slow stretching for the evening.

The full article covers what the review found, how much to trust it, the best time of day to exercise, a simple weekly plan and when to see a doctor about sleepless nights.
