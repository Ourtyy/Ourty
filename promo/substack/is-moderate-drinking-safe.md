For years, a glass of red wine with dinner had a healthy halo. A large study published this year adds to the growing evidence that the halo doesn't hold up.

Researchers reviewed more than 7,200 scientific papers and combined them with large US health datasets. They found no level of drinking that improved overall health. Any small possible benefit for the heart was outweighed by higher risks of cancer, liver disease and injuries, and risk rose more steeply above about one drink a day.

That doesn't mean a festive toast is off-limits. It means alcohol is best treated as an occasional treat, not a wellness habit.

**Three easy ways to drink a little less this season:**

- **Decide before you go.** Pick your number before the party starts, not halfway through it.
- **Alternate.** Have a sparkling water or an alcohol-free drink between alcoholic ones.
- **Go smaller.** A small glass of wine instead of a large one makes a real difference over a week.

The full article explains what the study found, what it doesn't say, why older studies made moderate drinking look healthy, and when it's worth talking to a doctor.
