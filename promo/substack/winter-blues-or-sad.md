Dark by late afternoon, hard to get out of bed, craving carbs all day? You're not imagining it. Shorter days really can affect your mood.

For many people it's the mild "winter blues". For some, though, it's seasonal affective disorder (SAD), a real form of depression that comes back every winter and gets in the way of daily life. Knowing which one you're dealing with helps you find the right support.

**Three simple habits that help:**

- **Get outside in daylight every day.** Even a cloudy sky is far brighter than indoor lighting.
- **Keep a steady sleep routine.** It's tempting to hibernate, but oversleeping can leave you groggier.
- **Stay connected.** Make plans with people you love instead of cancelling them.

The full article explains the signs of SAD, why less light affects your body clock, what the evidence says about those popular light therapy lamps, and when it's time to talk to a doctor.
