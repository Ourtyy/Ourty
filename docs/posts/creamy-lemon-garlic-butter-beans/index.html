Creamy Lemon Garlic Butter Beans: The 20-Minute BeanTok Dinner

Beans are all over TikTok. This creamy, cream-free butter bean skillet with spinach, garlic and lemon is a cheap, high-fibre dinner ready in 20 minutes.

By The Ourty Editorial Team · November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

If your feed has been full of people proudly stirring big pots of beans, you're not imagining it. #BeanTok is one of the food trends of the year, with creators urging everyone to eat more beans, some even aiming for two cups a day. And of all the beans getting attention, big, buttery, soft butter beans (also called lima beans) are the stars.

The good news? Unlike many viral food trends, this one is built on solid nutrition. The even better news: you don't need a "protocol" to enjoy it. You just need one great recipe. Here's ours: a creamy, garlicky butter bean skillet with spinach and lemon that gets its silky sauce from the beans themselves, no cream required.

Why beans are having a moment

The bean trend mixes three things people care about right now: gut health, fibre and food prices. Beans are cheap, keep for months in the cupboard and are packed with fibre and plant protein. As grocery bills stay high, a can of beans looks a lot more appealing than an expensive superfood powder.

The NHS recommends adults eat 30g of fibre a day, but most people only manage around 20g. Beans are one of the easiest ways to close that gap: just half a cup of black beans has around 8g of fibre.

What the evidence says about beans

Harvard's Nutrition Source sums up a large body of research:

  • Heart health: In one meta-analysis, eating legumes about four times a week was linked to a 14% lower risk of coronary heart disease. Clinical trials found pulses lowered LDL ("bad") cholesterol by around 6%.
  • Weight: In a survey of over 8,000 US adults, people who ate beans had 22% lower odds of obesity and smaller waistlines.
  • Diabetes: The evidence here is mixed, so treat big claims with caution.

Most of this research is observational, meaning it shows links rather than proof. But beans fit perfectly into the kind of fibre-rich, plant-forward eating that health experts have recommended for years.

A word on "two cups a day"

Some BeanTok creators promote a strict "bean protocol" of two cups daily. That's a lot of fibre to add overnight. A UK dietitian quoted in coverage of the trend called it a "huge increase all at once" that could upset your digestion. Our advice: start with a portion a few times a week and build up slowly, drinking plenty of water as you go. Your gut bacteria need time to adjust, and the gas does usually settle.

Creamy Lemon Garlic Butter Beans

Serves: 2 as a main, 4 as a side Time: 20 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 small onion or 2 shallots, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • A pinch of chilli flakes (optional)
  • 2 cans (400g each) butter beans, drained and rinsed
  • 200ml vegetable stock
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 2 big handfuls of baby spinach (about 100g)
  • Zest and juice of 1 lemon
  • 2 tbsp grated Parmesan or 1 tbsp nutritional yeast (optional)
  • Salt and black pepper
  • A small handful of fresh parsley or basil, to serve

Method

  1. Soften the onion. Warm the olive oil in a large frying pan over medium heat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 4–5 minutes until soft and golden at the edges.
  2. Add the flavour. Stir in the garlic, smoked paprika and chilli flakes and cook for 1 minute, until fragrant. Don't let the garlic brown.
  3. Make the creamy base. Add the beans and stock. Simmer for 3 minutes, then use the back of a fork or a potato masher to crush about a quarter of the beans against the side of the pan. This is the secret: the mashed beans thicken the stock into a silky, creamy sauce.
  4. Add the tomatoes. Stir in the cherry tomatoes and simmer for 4–5 minutes more, until they start to burst and the sauce coats the back of a spoon. Add a splash of water if it gets too thick.
  5. Wilt the greens. Fold in the spinach a handful at a time until just wilted.
  6. Finish bright. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the lemon zest, most of the lemon juice and the Parmesan, if using. Taste and adjust with salt, pepper and more lemon.
  7. Serve. Scatter with fresh herbs and a drizzle of olive oil. Serve with crusty wholegrain bread, over rice or quinoa, or topped with a fried egg.

Tip: Rinsing canned beans in a sieve under cold water removes the starchy liquid and some of the salt. Choose cans labelled "no added salt" when you can, and season to taste at the end.

Easy variations

  • Extra protein: Stir in a can of tuna or some flaked salmon at the end, or crack two eggs into little wells in the sauce, cover and cook for 5 minutes.
  • Sun-dried tomato style: Swap the cherry tomatoes for 4–5 chopped sun-dried tomatoes and add a splash of milk or oat milk for an even richer sauce.
  • Greens swap: Use chopped kale or cavolo nero instead of spinach; add it with the tomatoes so it has time to soften.
  • Herby and green: Finish with a spoonful of pesto instead of Parmesan.
  • Make it a soup: Double the stock and blend half for a creamy white bean soup.

Storage

Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The sauce thickens as it cools, so add a splash of water or stock when reheating. It also makes a great packed lunch, spooned over toast or stirred into pasta.

The bottom line

The BeanTok trend gets one big thing right: most of us would benefit from eating more beans. They're cheap, filling, rich in fibre and linked to better heart health. You don't need to follow a strict two-cups-a-day rule; just build them into meals you genuinely enjoy, and increase slowly if you're not used to them. This creamy lemon garlic skillet is an easy, delicious place to start.
