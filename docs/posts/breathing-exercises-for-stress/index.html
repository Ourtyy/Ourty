A 5-Minute Breathing Exercise for Stressful Days

A simple, free technique you can use anywhere to feel calmer — step by step.

By The Ourty Editorial Team · November 8, 2026 · 2 min read

When stress hits, your breathing often becomes fast and shallow without you noticing. The good news is that this works both ways: by slowing your breathing on purpose, you can help your body calm down.

This exercise takes about five minutes and you can do it at your desk, on the bus or in bed.

Why breathing helps

When we feel threatened or under pressure, the body's "fight or flight" response kicks in: heart rate rises and breathing quickens. Slow, deep breathing sends the opposite signal and can help you feel more relaxed. The NHS recommends breathing exercises as a simple way to manage stress, anxiety and panic.

The exercise, step by step

You can do this sitting, standing or lying down. Wherever you are, try to get comfortable and loosen any tight clothing.

  1. Get comfortable. If you're sitting, place your feet flat on the floor. If you're lying down, let your arms rest a little away from your body.
  2. Breathe in gently through your nose. Let the breath flow deep into your belly without forcing it. Count slowly from one to five.
  3. Breathe out through your mouth. Again, without forcing it, count from one to five.
  4. Keep going for 3 to 5 minutes. Breathe in and out in a steady rhythm.

Tip: place one hand on your chest and one on your belly. When you breathe in, the hand on your belly should rise more than the one on your chest.

Don't worry if you can't reach a count of five at first. Start with three and build up.

Make it a habit

Like most skills, breathing exercises work best with practice. Try doing this once or twice a day when you're already calm — for example, when you wake up or before bed. Then it will feel natural when you really need it.

Some ideas for building the habit:

  • Pair it with something you already do, like your morning coffee
  • Set a gentle reminder on your phone
  • Use the time waiting for a kettle to boil or a page to load

Other quick ways to ease stress

Breathing is a great tool, but it works even better alongside other healthy habits:

  • A short walk outside
  • Talking to a friend
  • Writing down what's worrying you
  • Cutting back on caffeine if you feel jittery

When to get support

Breathing exercises are a helpful self-care tool, but they aren't a replacement for professional help. If stress or anxiety is affecting your daily life, sleep or relationships, speak to your doctor or a mental health professional. If you're in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line straight away.
