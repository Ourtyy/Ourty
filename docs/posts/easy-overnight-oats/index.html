Easy Overnight Oats: A 5-Minute High-Fibre Breakfast

A no-cook breakfast you prepare the night before — plus four flavour ideas and why fibre matters.

By The Ourty Editorial Team · November 8, 2026 · 2 min read

If mornings are rushed, overnight oats are a lifesaver. You mix everything in a jar before bed, and breakfast is ready when you wake up. No cooking, no washing up a pan, and it's packed with fibre.

Why fibre matters

Most of us don't eat enough fibre. The NHS recommends adults aim for 30 grams of fibre a day, but the average adult gets around 20 grams. Fibre helps keep your digestion regular, keeps you feeling full for longer, and diets high in fibre are linked to a lower risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer.

Oats are a great source — and they contain a type of soluble fibre called beta-glucan.

Basic recipe (1 serving)

You'll need:

  • 50 g (½ cup) rolled oats
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) milk, or a plant-based alternative
  • 2 tablespoons plain or Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tablespoon chia seeds (optional, for extra fibre)
  • A handful of fruit
  • A drizzle of honey or maple syrup (optional)

Method:

  1. Put the oats, milk, yoghurt and chia seeds in a jar or container and stir well.
  2. Cover and leave in the fridge overnight, or for at least 4 hours.
  3. In the morning, give it a stir. Add a splash more milk if it's too thick.
  4. Top with fruit and a little honey if you like.

That's it! It keeps in the fridge for up to 2 days, so you can make a couple at once.

Four flavour ideas

  • Berry boost: frozen mixed berries stirred in the night before — they defrost into a lovely sauce.
  • Apple pie: grated apple, a pinch of cinnamon and a few chopped walnuts.
  • Banana & peanut butter: sliced banana and a spoonful of peanut butter on top.
  • Tropical: mango chunks, a little desiccated coconut and a squeeze of lime.

Tip: to keep added sugar low, let the fruit do the sweetening. Ripe bananas and berries add plenty of natural sweetness.

Make it work for you

  • Dairy-free? Use oat, soya or almond milk and a plant-based yoghurt.
  • Need gluten-free? Oats are naturally gluten-free but are often contaminated with wheat, so choose oats labelled gluten-free.
  • Want more protein? Use Greek yoghurt or add a spoonful of nut butter.

The bottom line

Overnight oats are a cheap, quick and filling way to start the day, and a simple step towards the recommended 30 grams of fibre. Make a jar tonight — your future self will thank you.
