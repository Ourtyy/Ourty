Winter Citrus Salad with Kale and Chickpeas: A Bright Vitamin C Bowl

A colourful winter citrus salad with oranges, pomegranate, kale and chickpeas. Easy 15-minute recipe, plus what vitamin C really does (and doesn't) do.

By The Ourty Editorial Team · November 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Just as the days turn short and grey, the shops fill up with the brightest fruit of the year. Oranges, blood oranges, clementines and grapefruit are at their juiciest in winter, and they make the perfect antidote to heavy, beige comfort food.

This salad puts them centre stage: sweet citrus slices, ruby pomegranate seeds and soft massaged kale, with crunchy roasted chickpeas and a zesty dressing. It takes about 15 minutes, looks stunning on the table, and packs a generous dose of vitamin C, fibre and plant protein. Below, you'll also find what vitamin C actually does for you — and where the hype goes too far.

Why a citrus salad makes sense in winter

Vitamin C every day

Your body can't store vitamin C, so you need some from food every day, according to the NHS. The good news is that most people can get all they need from their diet. Citrus is the classic source, but peppers, strawberries, kiwi, broccoli, Brussels sprouts and even potatoes count too.

How much? The NHS puts the adult need at 40mg a day, while US guidelines quoted by Harvard's Nutrition Source suggest 75mg for women and 90mg for men. Either way, a couple of portions of vitamin C-rich fruit and vegetables a day makes it easy to get there.

Raw is best for vitamin C

Vitamin C is fragile. Harvard notes that heat, light and long cooking break it down, and because it dissolves in water, it can leak into cooking liquid. That's one reason a raw salad is such a smart way to enjoy it.

It helps you absorb iron from plants

Here's a lesser-known bonus: vitamin C helps your body absorb the type of iron found in plant foods, like leafy greens and beans. That's why this salad pairs citrus with kale and chickpeas — the orange helps you get more from the greens and legumes in the same bowl.

The recipe: Winter citrus salad with kale and crispy chickpeas

Serves: 2 as a main, 4 as a side Time: 15 minutes (plus 20 minutes if you roast the chickpeas)

Ingredients

For the salad

  • 1 large bunch of kale (about 150g), tough stems removed
  • 1 tsp olive oil and a pinch of salt, for massaging
  • 3 mixed citrus fruits (for example 1 orange, 1 blood orange, 1 pink grapefruit or 2 clementines)
  • Seeds from ½ pomegranate
  • 1 can (400g) chickpeas, drained, rinsed and patted dry
  • ½ small red onion, very thinly sliced
  • A small handful of toasted walnuts or pumpkin seeds
  • Optional: crumbled feta or a few torn mint leaves

For the dressing

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tbsp fresh orange juice (squeeze it from the citrus offcuts)
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp honey or maple syrup
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar or lemon juice
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Crisp the chickpeas (optional but worth it). Toss the dried chickpeas with a little olive oil, a pinch of salt and smoked paprika. Roast at 200°C (180°C fan) for 20–25 minutes, shaking once, until golden and crunchy.
  2. Massage the kale. Tear the leaves into bite-size pieces. Add the olive oil and salt and scrunch with your hands for 1–2 minutes, until the leaves turn darker and softer.
  3. Prepare the citrus. Slice off the top and bottom of each fruit, then cut away the peel and white pith with a sharp knife, following the curve. Slice into rounds. Squeeze the leftover peel over a bowl to collect juice for the dressing.
  4. Soak the onion. Put the sliced onion in a little cold water or vinegar for 5 minutes to soften its bite, then drain.
  5. Whisk the dressing. Combine all the dressing ingredients in a jar and shake well. Taste and adjust.
  6. Assemble. Toss the kale with half the dressing and spread it on a platter. Arrange the citrus rounds on top, then scatter over the chickpeas, onion, pomegranate seeds and nuts. Drizzle with the rest of the dressing and add feta or mint if you like.

Tip: Dressed kale holds up well, so this salad keeps in the fridge for a day — just add the chickpeas and nuts right before serving so they stay crunchy.

Easy variations

  • Make it heartier: Add cooked quinoa, farro or lentils for a filling lunch bowl.
  • Swap the greens: Try rocket, shaved fennel, radicchio or thinly sliced Brussels sprouts — the last two are in season and add a pleasant bitterness.
  • Add creaminess: Sliced avocado or a spoon of thick yoghurt pairs beautifully with citrus.
  • Go savoury-sweet: Add a few olives or a sprinkle of chilli flakes for contrast.
  • Make it a side: Skip the chickpeas and serve alongside roast chicken or fish.

Vitamin C myths to leave behind

Citrus is wonderful, but it isn't magic. A few things worth knowing:

It won't stop you catching colds. Harvard's Nutrition Source says reviews have found that high-dose vitamin C supplements don't prevent colds in the average person. They may modestly shorten a cold or make it milder for some people — but a daily orange is not a force field.

More isn't better. The NHS warns that taking more than 1,000mg a day from supplements can cause stomach pain, diarrhoea and wind. Food is a far gentler way to get what you need. If you're thinking about supplements, talk to a doctor or pharmacist first.

About that new study. You may have seen headlines in November 2026 about vitamin C "blocking cancer-linked chemicals". The research, from the University of Waterloo, was a computer model of how vitamin C interacts with nitrates and nitrites in the gut. It suggests that eating vitamin C alongside meals might reduce the formation of some harmful compounds, especially with processed meats like bacon. That's an interesting idea, but it was a simulation, not a trial in people, and the authors say more research is needed. The practical takeaway is the same old good advice: eat plenty of fruit and vegetables with your meals.

The bottom line

A winter citrus salad is an easy, beautiful way to eat more fruit and vegetables when it's cold and dark outside. It's raw, so you keep the vitamin C; the citrus helps you absorb plant iron from the kale and chickpeas; and it tastes like sunshine. Just enjoy it as part of a varied diet rather than as cold-season armour — no single food can do that job alone.
