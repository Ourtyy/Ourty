How Much Exercise Do You Really Need? The 150-Minute Rule Explained

The World Health Organization's activity guidelines in plain English — and simple ways to hit them without a gym.

By The Ourty Editorial Team · November 8, 2026 · 2 min read

If you've ever wondered whether your daily walk "counts", you're not alone. The good news: the official recommendation is more achievable than most people think, and you don't need a gym membership to meet it.

The short answer

According to the World Health Organization, adults aged 18–64 should aim for:

  • 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity per week, or
  • 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity per week, or a mix of both
  • Muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week

That's about 22 minutes of brisk walking a day. Not bad at all.

What counts as "moderate" and "vigorous"?

A simple way to tell is the talk test:

  • Moderate: you can talk, but you can't sing. Think brisk walking, cycling on flat ground, dancing or gardening.
  • Vigorous: you can only say a few words before needing a breath. Think running, swimming laps, fast cycling or a game of football.

One minute of vigorous activity is roughly worth two minutes of moderate activity, which is why the vigorous target is half as long.

Don't forget your muscles

Cardio gets most of the attention, but strength matters too — especially as we age, when muscle mass naturally declines. Muscle-strengthening doesn't have to mean lifting heavy weights. These all count:

  • Bodyweight exercises like squats, push-ups (on your knees is fine) and lunges
  • Resistance bands
  • Carrying heavy shopping bags
  • Yoga or Pilates
  • Heavy gardening, like digging

Every minute adds up

Older advice said activity only counted in blocks of 10 minutes or more. The WHO's 2020 guidelines dropped that rule: all movement counts. Taking the stairs, walking to the shop or a quick dance in the kitchen all chip away at your weekly total.

Tip: if 150 minutes feels like a lot, start where you are. Some activity is better than none, and the WHO notes that even small increases bring health benefits.

Sitting less matters too

The guidelines also recommend limiting the time we spend sitting. Long periods of inactivity are linked to poorer health, even in people who exercise. Try standing up and moving for a couple of minutes every half hour — set a reminder on your phone if it helps.

A simple weekly plan

Here's one realistic way to hit the targets:

  1. Monday, Wednesday, Friday: 30-minute brisk walk
  2. Tuesday and Thursday: 20 minutes of bodyweight strength exercises
  3. Saturday: 60-minute bike ride, hike or swim
  4. Sunday: rest, or a gentle stroll

That adds up to 150 minutes of moderate activity plus two strength sessions.

The bottom line

You don't need to train like an athlete. A little over 20 minutes of brisk movement a day, plus some strength work twice a week, meets the global recommendation. Start small, pick activities you enjoy, and build from there.
