Sleep is often the first thing we sacrifice when life gets busy. But it isn't a luxury — it's as important for your health as food and exercise. So how much do you actually need?

## The recommendation

The [US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html), based on guidance from sleep medicine experts, recommends that **adults aged 18 to 60 get 7 or more hours of sleep per night**. Adults over 60 generally need 7 to 9 hours.

Yet surveys suggest around a third of adults regularly get less than this.

## Why it matters

While you sleep, your body and brain are hard at work: consolidating memories, repairing tissue and regulating hormones. Regularly sleeping too little has been linked to a higher risk of:

- Weight gain
- Type 2 diabetes
- High blood pressure and heart disease
- Low mood and anxiety
- Accidents caused by tiredness

In the short term, even one bad night can affect your concentration, reaction time and mood.

## Quality matters as much as quantity

Eight hours in bed doesn't help much if you wake up constantly. Signs you may not be sleeping well include feeling tired after a full night, needing caffeine to get through the morning, or nodding off during the day.

## Seven habits for better sleep

1. **Keep a regular schedule.** Go to bed and wake up at roughly the same time every day — weekends included.
2. **Make your bedroom dark, quiet and cool.** Blackout curtains, earplugs or an eye mask can help.
3. **Put screens away before bed.** Phones and laptops keep your brain alert. Try to switch off 30–60 minutes before sleeping.
4. **Watch the caffeine.** Coffee, tea, cola and energy drinks can stay in your system for hours. Many experts suggest avoiding them in the afternoon and evening.
5. **Go easy on alcohol.** It may make you drowsy, but it disrupts sleep later in the night.
6. **Get daylight in the morning.** Natural light helps set your body clock.
7. **Move during the day.** Regular physical activity can help you fall asleep faster — just avoid intense exercise right before bed.

> **Can't sleep?** If you've been lying awake for around 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light (like reading a paper book), and return to bed when you feel sleepy.

## When to see a doctor

Talk to a healthcare professional if poor sleep lasts for weeks, affects your daily life, or if someone tells you that you snore loudly or stop breathing during sleep — this can be a sign of sleep apnoea, a common and treatable condition.

## The bottom line

Most adults need at least 7 hours of good-quality sleep a night. A consistent routine, a sleep-friendly bedroom and a little less screen time in the evening can make a real difference.
